MODULE 00 Briefing

What you're joining, in sixty seconds

If you read nothing else today, read this card.

  • Nucleus is building a personal AI that continuously fine-tunes a model on the user's own life. Your life doesn't scroll out of a context window — it compounds into weights, every night.
  • Recording is the head of that pipeline. Nothing sits upstream of us except the user. We capture their physical and digital life (phone, browser tab, mac screen and mic; a wearable body-cam later), and we deliver it downstream with zero silent loss — every gap known, every byte timestamped and attributable to a device.
  • We speak exactly one outward contract: C1. Bytes go to storage /raw first, then a small JSON "envelope" tells data-processing where they landed.
  • We never interpret the stream. No transcription, no captioning, no enrichment — that is data-processing's job. We are transport, integrity, and honesty.
  • We are also the privacy front line: pause / mute / delete-last-N-minutes are chartered as first-class deliverables. They are deliberately deferred right now (D13) because the only users are consenting teammates — but the design keeps a seat open for them.
  • The service is alpha-complete for computer capture: three real clients, a checked loss guarantee, and a live fleet on node-7. The wearable, consent enforcement, and fleet-scale telemetry are the open road ahead.
How to read this guide

Modules 01–03 are the foundations (why, the words, the wire). 04–07 go inside the machine. 08–10 are the judgment layer — why things are the way they are, and what's honestly open. 11 gets you running code. Take module 12's quiz when you're done; it covers the traps that bite real sessions.

MODULE 01 The big picture

Why Nucleus exists, and where recording sits

You can't reason about our design choices without the company's one big idea.

The one big idea: context becomes weights

Every AI assistant today meets its user fresh. Context windows grew from 128k to 1M tokens, but a window is still a ceiling — your history gets lossily compressed and hopefully survives the next session. And the model itself is identical for everyone; only prompts differ.

Nucleus's bet: a whole human life is about 876K hours of perception (24 h × 365 d × 100 y). Frontier models pre-train on millions of hours — a single life fits. So take an open vision-language model (Qwen3-VL-32B, decision D6), give each user their own LoRA adapter, and fine-tune it nightly on what they actually lived that day. The model that answers you tomorrow morning remembers yesterday, because it was trained on it overnight — the memory lives in the weights rather than in a prompt.

Two loops, one system

Everything in v0 is one of two loops sharing the same stores and the same per-user model:

LEARN LOOP · background, nightly — this is our loop
User's life→ RECORDING→ C1 → Data-Processing
ASR · captions · OCR→ C2 → Storage /context→ C10 → Continuum
nightly fine-tune→ C5 → Model directory
SERVE LOOP · interactive, seconds — not ours, but know it exists
User asks→ Input / QueryBuilder→ C3 → Inference
vLLM + user's LoRA→ C9 → Output→ User

The founders' framing: the serve loop makes the product usable today; the learn loop is why it exists. We are the first mile of the learn loop. If we drop a chunk of someone's day silently, the model simply never learns that part of their life — and nobody would know it's missing. That's why our headline guarantee is "zero silent loss": loss can happen (networks fail, browsers die), but it must always be detectable and named, never quiet.

Boundary you must respect

Interactive chat requests (a user asking their AI something) go through the input service, never through us. We carry only the passive, always-on life stream. The charter marks C3/C8 as "boundary markers" — if someone proposes routing a chat payload through /capture/*, the answer is no.

MODULE 02 Vocabulary

Speak recording before you read recording

Five words carry this whole service, and two of them look interchangeable until they bite you. Click each card.

segmentclient → server

The upload unit: a ~10-second, self-contained media clip a client POSTs to /capture/segments. Its counter is segment_num, dense per capture.

Self-contained matters: each segment is a complete playable file, so an offline queue can retry any one of them alone, in any order — losing one never poisons the rest.

chunkserver → DP

The emission unit: one single-modality piece (one /raw blob + one C1 envelope). The server demuxes each A/V segment into an audio chunk and a video chunk. Its counter is sequence, dense per stream.

Segment ≠ chunk. One segment typically becomes two chunks.

streamcrosses services

One continuous single-modality flow from one device capture — "this phone's audio", "this tab's video". stream_id is a ULID, globally unique, and is the identity that crosses service boundaries. Gap detection lives inside a stream: (stream_id, sequence) must be dense.

capturenever travels past C1

One start→stop on a device: press-record→stop, CLI run→Ctrl-C, extension click→click. First-class in our ledger as capture_id; it never crosses the C1 wire (C1 carries stream_id).

Until 2026-07-29 this was a capture session with a session_id — renamed because that collided with the serve loop's chat session_id (C3/C4). The collision is gone; older docs' dated entries still use the old words.

recorddownstream artifact

One row in storage /context conforming to the C2 contract — a transcript, a caption, an OCR pass. Data-processing makes them; we never do.

When DP processes a chunk inline, its reply to our /ingest push lists the record_ids it wrote — the only place we ever see them, and we keep them in the ledger as provenance. Under async ingest the 202 accept carries none; the ids stay derivable rather than stored (a recorded D16 caveat).

envelopethe C1 payload

The metadata describing a captured chunk: who, which device and stream, when, and where the bytes live (blob_ref). The bytes themselves never ride the envelope — they're already durable in /raw before the envelope exists.

ledgerour memory

The SQLite database (var/ledger.db) tracking captures, segments, streams, and chunks. It stores bookkeeping, never the recordings themselves: which segments arrived, which chunks were sent onward, and what the downstream services acknowledged. The media bytes live in storage.

Because this bookkeeping is on disk, a crash loses nothing, and the gap report always has receipts to check claims against.

verdictclean | gaps | recording

The gap report's one-word answer for a capture. clean = every segment arrived, every chunk was delivered, and DP has finished processing each one — the C2 records are durably written in storage. An acknowledgement alone is never enough. gaps = something was lost, and the report names exactly what. recording = work still honestly in flight. There is no fourth word.

demuxsplit, don't interpret

Splitting a muxed A/V segment into per-modality chunk files with ffmpeg. Audio is re-encoded to 16 kHz mono WAV (the shape ASR natively wants); video is container-copied, never re-encoded. This is the one media transformation we're allowed: we change the packaging, and interpreting the content (transcribing, captioning) stays data-processing's job.

spoolbytes in transit

The on-disk staging dir where uploaded segment bytes wait between received (accepted by our server, acked to the client) and emitted (that segment's chunks delivered to both destinations — bytes in storage /raw, C1 envelope pushed to DP). Deleted after emit by default; RECORDING_KEEP_SPOOL=1 keeps it — that knob is the designed holdback point for the future consent gate (delete-last-N before anything leaves the device path).

Why two counters?

The client→server leg (segment_num per capture) and the server→DP leg (sequence per stream) are two separate continuity domains, joined by the ledger. A segment a phone dropped shows up as a client-leg hole; downstream C1 streams stay dense by construction, so the gap report is always honest about where a loss happened.

Why the stream can't just reuse segment_num: a segment can lack a modality — camera off for segment 1 means a video stream numbered by segments would read 0, 2, a hole that looks exactly like a lost chunk. And some sources (the headless carve, the replay plan, the future bodycam link) have no segments at all. Density is the loss alarm, so each counter lives in the namespace where density is guaranteed.

MODULE 03 The contract

C1 — the only promise we make

A contract is a pinned payload shape between two services. Ours is C1, and it has two legs. Click any field in the envelope to see why it exists.

Two legs, always in this order

1 · Blob leg — bytes first

We PUT the raw bytes to storage /raw/blobs. Storage mints an opaque blob_ref — a claim ticket saying "the bytes are at this locker", idempotent on chunk_id. Bytes go first, always: the envelope announcing a chunk is only sent after storage confirms the deposit, so a reference can never point at bytes that don't exist. The announcement is proof of deposit.

2 · Envelope leg — then the announcement

We push the JSON envelope to data-processing POST /ingest: "here are a few seconds of this user's life, and here is where I put the bytes." Delivery is at-least-once; DP dedups on chunk_id.

The envelope, annotated

← click a field

Every field earned its place through a decision or a bug. The detail panel explains each one.

The four rules a builder must honour

  • Blob first. The bytes are durable in /raw before the envelope is emitted. And because bytes in /raw can be legitimately deleted later (delete-last-N, right-to-be-forgotten), consumers treat "these bytes are gone" as a normal answer.
  • At-least-once, dedup on chunk_id. Retries re-send the identical request; both storage and DP are idempotent on chunk_id, so duplicates have exactly-once effect.
  • sequence is dense, zero-based, +1 per chunk inside a globally-unique stream_id. Any break — including a first-seen value that isn't 0 — means lost chunks. This is the "zero silent loss" mechanism. Ordering and identity are separate jobs — the dedup key is chunk_id.
  • t_start/t_end are RFC3339 UTC — the canonical instant, the axis every downstream alignment uses. The device's timezone rides beside them (module 07), never instead of them.
Prototype posture (D19)

The repo deliberately avoids the word "frozen" for contracts: a pinned shape is "stable enough to build against today", never immutable. But changing C1 is not a recording decision — it edits ARCHITECTURE.md §Contracts first, then contracts/*.json, then both owning services' canvases, ratified at the founders' board. Cheap to change ≠ unowned.

MODULE 04 The capture surfaces

Three clients, one client-agnostic wire

All three speak the exact same /capture/* wire. The wire is client-agnostic: a new capture surface requires zero server changes.

What it is: a static, no-build-step web page (clients/web/) served by our own server at /client/, reached from a phone over an HTTPS tunnel. Mic + camera via getUserMedia. It's the bodycam stand-in and the beta tester's press-record surface.

segmenting recorder restart every ~10 s (D-M1-1)
media mp4 H.264+AAC on iOS Safari
device_id phone-web-<suffix> — suffix minted once, saved in the browser's localStorage, so the same phone keeps the same id
upload one serialized queue, in-order by construction
end marker sent on Stop; if the page closes first, the browser's sendBeacon API delivers it as a last gasp
keep-alive screen wake lock while recording

The decision that shaped it: D-M1-1

MediaRecorder's timeslice fragments are not self-contained (only the first carries the container header, and iOS is unreliable with timeslice). So the client restarts the recorder every 10 s — each stop yields a standalone playable blob = one durable upload unit. Cost, stated honestly: a ~tens-of-ms capture gap at each restart. The data records it truthfully — segment_num stays dense and the wall-clock spans carry the real gap (t_end[n] < t_start[n+1] by the restart amount).

Failure honesty

The upload queue is in-memory: a page reload loses queued segments, and the ledger flags exactly which ones (client-leg missing). Retry is forever on network/5xx with exponential backoff (1 s·2ⁿ, cap 30 s); a 4xx is a client bug — surfaced, never retried. IndexedDB persistence is a named later hardening.

What it is: a Chrome MV3 extension (clients/extension/) that records the active browser tab — video + audio in one muxed webm (vp8+opus) stream via chrome.tabCapture. A deliberately passive surface: no content scripts, no DOM access, no static host permissions — it can see nothing about the page except its pixels and sound.

The pivot worth learning from: D-E7

The original design used Chrome's desktop screen-picker (desktopCapture). It survived unit tests, adversarial review, and two skeptic rounds — then failed three times on the tester's real browser (Comet). The picker's window list showed only its own window; "Entire Screen" errored; the cross-context stream-id handoff proved inherently fragile. Instead of another patch, the team deleted the whole picker path and pivoted to tabCapture, the most stable Chromium capture primitive. It worked on the first real run.

Accepted trade-off

The extension captures a browser tab, not the whole desktop. Full-screen capture is the mac CLI's job. Escape hatch worth knowing: a recording pointed at a bad server URL retries forever by design — the Discard unsent button (draining state only) is how a user bails out.

What it is: a single-file, stdlib-only Python CLI (clients/mac/nucleus_capture.py, ~725 lines). ffmpeg's avfoundation captures screen + mic, cuts ~10 s self-contained mp4 segments into a spool dir, and a Python uploader thread speaks the same wire. Full-desktop capture lives here.

deps python3 ≥3.9 + brew install ffmpeg, nothing else
timestamps duration-chained from a wall-clock anchor (D-F2)
completeness a segment uploads once the next numbered file exists (D-F4)
device_id mac-cli-<suffix> in ~/.nucleus/
stop Ctrl-C once = graceful drain; twice = polite abandon
exit codes 0 clean · 2 gaps · 1 error

Three clever bits

  • Duration chaining (D-F2): anchor = wall-clock at capture start; then t_end[n] = t_start[n] + duration[n] (ffprobe per segment), t_start[n+1] = t_end[n]. Capture IS continuous here (unlike the phone's restart gaps), so exact adjacency is honest — and it doubles as a continuity signal.
  • --source test (D-F3): ffmpeg can synthesize media out of thin air — a moving test-pattern video and a pure tone. This flag swaps only the capture input for those generators; the encoding, 10-second segmenting, uploading and server demux are the identical code path a real screen recording takes. It's how our headless dev server (no screen, no mic) E2E-tests the client, and how a mac user smoke-tests the connection before granting any permissions.
  • Upload-when-finished (D-F4): the CLI must never upload a file ffmpeg is still writing. It leans on a guarantee of ffmpeg's segment writer: file N+1 is only opened after file N is finished, so "the next numbered file exists" proves the previous one is complete — and when ffmpeg exits, everything left is final. No filesystem watchers, no size-polling guesswork.

Alpha war story

First real run: zero segments ever finalized. The screen device refused -framerate 15, ffmpeg derived a garbage output rate, duplicated frames endlessly, and the segment muxer — which cuts on media time — never reached 10 media-seconds. Fix: pin the output rate with an fps= filter regardless of what the device negotiates. Also observed and documented: segment 0 spans ~18 s (device warm-up — audio flows before the first video frame). First-segment-only; the time axis stays continuous.

What it is: the server's own capture modes — no human device involved.

  • POST /capture/run — the original M0 path: carve a continuous WAV (or a synthetic tone) into chunks and emit them through the same blob-first + C1 path. It's how the learn loop gets smoke-tested, and where VAD-cut chunking lives (module 05).
  • The replay source (app/sources/replay_source.py) — re-emits an already-recorded day of audio from a plan file, chunk by chunk, with the plan's original timestamps. Built for Phase-3 dogfooding: the rest of the pipeline cannot tell it apart from a live capture, which is exactly the property the experiment needed. One plan = one capture = one stream, dense sequence — what an always-on capture actually looks like.
Status — what stays, what retires

The replay source is a validation rig: no production path hits it, and it stays useful for pipeline regression. The /capture/run endpoint is dev and smoke tooling and may eventually retire. The carve machinery underneath them is different — VAD-cut chunking is the ratified production design (D-M1-2) for any source where the server owns a continuous feed, which is exactly the future bodycam device link. Test paths on top; the front half of M3 underneath.

The seam that makes new surfaces cheap

Both are ChunkSources — the registry pattern in app/sources/__init__.py. A source owns modality concerns (how a stream is captured and carved, each chunk's wall-clock span); the emit path owns stream concerns (stream_id minting, dense sequence, hashing, blob-first PUT, validated C1 push). Adding a modality = drop one new file + register one line. The emit path and the C1 wire never change. This is the seam a future wearable or webcam source plugs into.

How the verdict is computed

The report joins both legs plus a live DP cross-check (GET /continuity/{stream_id} on data-processing, 2 s budget, best-effort):

clean      = capture ended
           ∧ no client-leg hole (missing_count == 0)
           ∧ every received segment emitted (none failed)
           ∧ every DP-checked stream: nothing missing-unacked,
             nothing dead-lettered, nothing merely "accepted"

recording  = not ended, or work honestly in flight
gaps       = anything lost — and the report names exactly what

Read that carefully: clean is earned, never assumed. The report is also honest about what it could not check: each stream's DP block carries a checked flag. checked: true means data-processing answered the live continuity question; checked: false means it was unreachable, and the report makes no claim in either direction. Absence of evidence is reported as exactly that, never turned into a verdict.

MODULE 06 Hard-won correctness

The async seam, and why crashes lose nothing

Two designs here look fussy until you see the failure they close. Both are ratified decisions — D16 at the founders' board, R-2 locally — each with a named failure mode it exists to kill.

The 202 problem

In inline mode — today's fleet default — DP does all of its work inside our /ingest request, and the 200 {record_ids} reply only comes back once the records are durably written. That couples our capture cadence to DP's processing time: transcription for audio, captioning and OCR for video, whatever the pipeline runs for that chunk. DP's async mode (INGEST_ASYNC) decouples the two — DP answers 202 {accepted} immediately and does the work on a worker pool.

But a 202 opens a silent-loss window: DP said "got it", then could crash before processing. If we treated "accepted" as "done", a lost chunk would read clean forever. The rule that closes it — agreed at the founders' board and now part of the wire's contract (D16):

The invariant (decision D16)

dp_acked = 1 ⇔ the C2 record is durably written. An accepted-but-unconfirmed chunk keeps dp_acked = 0, dp_state = 'accepted' — and the verdict stays recording, never clean. Confirmation happens later, when DP's /continuity reports the chunk processed — and we persist that promotion, so a DP restart (whose in-memory sets are volatile) can never un-confirm what we know landed.

The safety valve is re-drive (POST /capture/captures/{id}/redrive): re-push each accepted-but-unconfirmed chunk's original C1 envelope and let DP's dedup sort out the truth. One of three things happens to each chunk:

  • DP already processed it — the push short-circuits to 200 {record_ids}, and we mark the chunk confirmed.
  • DP still has it queued — we get another 202, and the chunk simply stays "accepted" until the next check.
  • DP genuinely lost it — the push re-claims the chunk, and DP processes it now.

The bytes never travel again — they are already durable in /raw. Only the small envelope is re-sent.

status  INGEST_ASYNC is off by default — inline is the shipped path today. Nothing further needs deciding before the flip; what remains is operational: a founders' go, one re-drive drill run against the live fleet, and retiring the RECORDING_HTTP_TIMEOUT=120 override that inline mode needs (it reverts to 30 s). The item sits on the recording board's next-list.

Crash safety — the three-legged stool

1 · Spool before ledger

The ack contract is "spool + ledger row are durable". The spool is written first (atomic .part rename, content-addressed name). If the row committed first, a crash between the two would make the client's retry hit the duplicate branch and ack bytes that exist nowhere. Found by adversarial review, fixed, regression-tested.

2 · Identity before emit

chunk_id + sequence are minted and persisted in the ledger before the first emit attempt. A retry or a post-restart re-emit uses the same identity — idempotent downstream, no fabricated gaps, no double blobs.

3 · Re-enqueue at startup

On boot, reenqueue_pending() re-queues every segment still in state received (acked, never emitted). An ack-then-crash loses nothing. failed stays failed until an explicit /retry — failure stays a visible state that someone explicitly resolves.

One more reconciliation worth knowing

DP's continuity tracker was originally in-memory. A mid-capture DP restart made it report already-delivered chunks as a permanent leading gap — verdict gaps forever, falsely. The report now reconciles DP's "missing" claim against our own persisted ack receipts: a sequence counts as lost only if DP misses it AND we hold no confirmed ack (dp.missing_unacked). DP has since grown its own durable journal, so the guarantee is durable on both legs — and our reconciliation stays as belt-and-suspenders.

MODULE 07 Civil time

The device owns the fact of where you were

A timezone bug here doesn't crash anything. It silently trains a model on the wrong shape of someone's day. That's why this one decision (D17) touches four services.

The problem, from first principles

All timestamps are UTC — correct for ordering and range queries. But the nightly trainer renders a human-readable "day-log" with anchors like "On Tuesday, around 09:00–09:20 local time…". UTC alone can answer "the last 24 hours"; it cannot answer "the user's Tuesday morning". So beside every UTC instant, the system needs to know which timezone the moment was lived in — and only one party can know that.

The split that solves it

The fact — where the user physically was

Owned by the capturing device — the only thing that can know it. Every client reports device_tz (an IANA id like Asia/Tokyo) and device_utc_offset_minutes with each segment it uploads; the server copies them onto every chunk demuxed from that segment, and C1 carries them onward per chunk. In the browser clients this is a single line of code: Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone.

The policy — when the user's "night" is

Owned by storage, as the per-user profile value home_tz (contract C12). It schedules the nightly training cycle and serves as the fallback when a record carries no device zone. The user declares it themselves. An earlier design had the system guess it from the first device report; the founders reversed that (D19), because a guessed zone can never be corrected by the person who knows, while a declared one can. And it does not move when they travel.

The travel test decides everything. Say your home_tz is Los Angeles and the nightly training cycle fires at 4:00 AM home time — a fixed moment each day, so every training window covers exactly one 24-hour slice of your life. You fly to Tokyo, nine time zones ahead. Every segment you capture there carries device_tz: Asia/Tokyo, so the day-log renders those mornings as Tokyo mornings — the fact travels with you.

Now imagine home_tz had followed you instead. "4:00 AM" would suddenly mean 4:00 AM Tokyo time, which is nine hours earlier on the absolute clock. The night you land, the boundary fires 9 hours sooner than yesterday's boundary did — so that training window covers only 15 hours of your life. When you fly home and the zone snaps back, the opposite happens and one window stretches to 33 hours. Two distorted "days" would enter the model's weights for no reason except bookkeeping. Keeping home_tz declared and unmoving means every window stays a clean 24 hours, while the Tokyo mornings still render correctly from the per-segment facts. Correct under travel, by construction.

What recording enforces at the edge

  • PST is rejected with a 400. Abbreviations are ambiguous and DST-sensitive; IANA ids only. Rejecting at the capture edge beats a renderer blowing up weeks later, far from the client that sent it. (Live-verified: Asia/Tokyo accepted and persisted; PST → 400.)
  • Absent is honest. A client that can't know its zone omits the fields entirely — no null, no guess — and downstream falls back to the declared home_tz. The same discipline covers old ledger rows written before clients reported zones: they stay empty rather than being backfilled, because the only guess available would be the timezone of the machine our server runs on — and that says where the server lives, never where the user was.
  • The offset rides along because it is not derivable from the zone: it's the independent witness of what the device believed, the only way to detect stale tzdata after the fact.
  • Re-driven chunks carry their original zone — the emitter joins the segments table so a re-push can never re-derive a fresher, wrong zone.
Why this module is in a recording guide

Because we're the origin. The zone travels C1 → C2 (data-processing copies it verbatim, no timezone logic allowed there) → storage columns → continuum's renderer. Four services carry it; only our clients can create it. If a new capture surface forgets its one line of timezone code, the whole chain degrades to fallback — silently correct-looking, subtly wrong under travel.

Where our choices ripple into other services

→ Data-Processing
  • Demux shape is DP's ask: audio lands as 16 kHz mono WAV because Whisper, pyannote and AST are all 16 kHz-native — a higher rate buys the models nothing (charter OQ3, resolved jointly). Video is container-copied to avoid generational loss.
  • VAD pairing: our pause-aligned cuts pair with DP's VAD gate before ASR (silence → honest empty transcript, kills Whisper's silence hallucination).
  • Our gap report queries DP live (/continuity) — their break/dup detector is the other half of our loss guarantee.
  • ASR language pinning exists because our real phone capture surfaced Whisper hallucinating Hindi/Korean script on faint room audio → DP grew ASR_LANGUAGE.
→ Storage
  • We write /raw; storage owns it (D11). Blob-first ordering is what lets storage mint blob_ref idempotently on chunk_id.
  • Production lean (OQ8, open): storage mints signed GCS URLs and clients upload directly — bytes bypass storage's process. Until then everything proxies through PUT /raw/blobs, which caps at tens-of-GB/day/user scale.
  • Our device_tz columns persist beside every UTC instant in /context — storage stores the fact we captured, and C12's home_tz is only the fallback.
  • Deletion: storage owns purge primitives for /raw; our delete-last-N (M2) will lean on them for anything already uploaded — the holdback buffer exists so it usually won't have to.
→ Continuum
  • Everything it trains on started here. The day-log's anchored blocks render from records whose spans and zones we stamped. A wrong t_start is a scene on the wrong Tuesday.
  • A silent capture gap = an un-trainable hole in the user's life the model never learns — discovered too late to re-capture. That's the mission sentence behind the verdict machinery.
  • Chunk granularity is a cost lever: escalation E-1 asks for --segment-seconds 10→60 — the single largest processing-cost lever measured (5.8×) — but it moves the audio leg too, so it's a joint call with DP-audio, parked with the founders.
  • The replay source exists for continuum's sake: Phase-3 proved the whole pipeline on a real recorded day by replaying it through our unmodified emit path.
Culture note — how decisions work here

A decision inside our chartered autonomy gets an R-number in services/recording/DECISIONS.md. Anything that re-cuts a charter, moves a contract, or binds a sibling is not ours to settle — it's proposed in the root HANDOFF.md §Escalations and ratified at a founders' session with a D-number. A decision is never rewritten to say something different; it's superseded by a new numbered row. When you see "D-E7" in a comment, you can trace exactly why the code is shaped that way.

MODULE 09 Observability

We emit; platform watches (once it builds the watcher)

D9's split: every service instruments itself and owns its dashboard; platform runs the one shared Prometheus + Grafana both founders open.

What we emit at /metrics

  • HTTP baseline: request rate by route/status + latency histograms — via a pure-ASGI middleware that never touches response bodies, with route templating so cardinality stays one series per route, not per session id.
  • Capture health (ledger-derived, at scrape time): segments by state, chunks by modality, chunks by DP state (accepted/processed/unemitted), captures total/active, client-leg missing + duplicate deliveries.
  • Pipeline: segment received→fully-emitted latency histogram; downstream retry counter per target service.

Choices worth copying

  • Zero dependencies: a ~300-line in-house registry + renderer (app/metrics.py) instead of pulling prometheus_client into the pinned requirements — the headless CI suite stays dependency-free.
  • Pull-time gauges: ledger counts are computed at scrape, memoized 1 s, so a scrape does one ledger pass instead of seven.
  • A metric must never break the request — every observation path is wrapped so telemetry can't fail the emit.

The dashboard (dashboards/recording.json) ships panels for capture health, emit latency p50/p95, downstream retries, request rates and 5xx. The catch: platform's shared backbone is still designed and unbuilt — nobody has a Grafana URL yet, which is exactly why our M6 milestone's exit criterion ("scraped by the shared Prometheus") stays open even though our emission half is done and tested. It's item 2 on the founders' board.

Open questions & escalations

E-1Segment length 10→60 s

The single largest cost lever measured: 5.8×. Moves the audio leg too → joint call with DP-audio, parked at the founders' board.

E-6Auto-retry of failed segments

A DP 503 is recoverable back-pressure, but our 4-attempt/0.25 s-backoff client makes it terminal in ~1.5 s — then waits for a manual /retry. A periodic re-drive of failed is the ask.

OQ1Wearable hardware pick

Off-the-shelf bodycam with an SDK vs a small custom build. Drives buffering, codec, and upload design. Decide before M3.

OQ2Delete-after-upload

v0 lean: an on-device holdback buffer (upload only after the delete window expires) so deletion never needs downstream cooperation.

OQ6Device identity & auth

Today device_ids are self-minted and nothing authenticates. Platform-owned identity with device-scoped tokens vs self-issued until platform exists.

OQ7Bystander policy

Two-party-consent jurisdictions, mute zones, indicator norms. Platform decides, we enforce. Needed before M3 wear tests.

OQ8Direct-to-GCS upload

The remaining piece of the /raw path: storage mints a signed URL, clients upload straight to GCS. Needed before tens-of-GB/day/user scale. (The demux half is built.)

OQ9Capture-everything vs gated

Research question: does VAD/motion gating starve the training signal continuum depends on? Joint experiment once fine-tuning runs on real streams.

D16Flip INGEST_ASYNC=1 on the fleet

The wire and re-drive path are built and drilled; making async the production default stays a founders' call.

D14Streaming ingest (additive leg)

Socket receiver → continuity buffer → server-side segmenter, terminating in the existing machinery. Build only when a surface needs sub-segment latency or bodycam firmware demands it.

ws-EDeferred surfaces

System/desktop audio for the extension; multi-tab simultaneous capture (feasible — the wire already supports N captures per device); Windows client if pilots need one (OQ5: pin the pilot OS fleet first).

ws-BClient hardening

IndexedDB persistence for the phone's in-memory upload queue; persisting final per-source verdicts in the extension popup.
