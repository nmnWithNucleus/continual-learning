MODULE 00 Briefing

What you're joining, in ninety seconds

If you read nothing else today, read this card.

The product. Nucleus is a personal AI whose defining bet is that a user's life context should not scroll away in a chat window — it should compound into the weights of a model that belongs to them. A wearable and computer capture record the user's day; every night, that day is fine-tuned into their personal adapter. The model that answers them tomorrow remembers today.

This service. Data Processing (DP) is the normalization layer between capture and everything else. Recording pushes us a few seconds of raw life at a time — a C1 envelope pointing at bytes in storage /raw. We pull the bytes, run them through a modality pipeline (ASR for audio, captioning + OCR for video), and write C2 processed records to storage /context: what was said, seen, and read — and exactly when.

Why it matters. The nightly trainer (continuum) never sees pixels or waveforms. It sees a day-log — text rendered from our records. If we transcribe it wrong, caption it thin, or timestamp it sloppily, the user's model learns a worse life. The charter says it plainly: the quality ceiling of the whole product is set here.

Where we stand. Audio is real end to end (faster-whisper with a VAD gate, verified on real capture). Video has two paths — a shipped per-keyframe legacy default and a newer clip path waiting on cutover gates. Image and text are deliberate stubs until a surface produces them. The service survives kill -9, dedups redeliveries, and refuses to lie about loss. 788 tests hold that in place.

How to read this guideModules 01–05 are the fundamentals — read them in order; each builds on the last. Modules 06–10 go deep on the machinery. 11–15 are the context around the code: decisions, what's open, and where you come in. Interactive panels are safe to click — nothing here touches a real service. One standing rule about trust: this page is a teaching view the founders sanctioned as the one allowed restatement of the repo (D22), and it pays for that by being corrected in the same session as any change it teaches. If you ever catch it disagreeing with the charter, the boards, or the code, the repo is right — and the fix belongs here, tracked in onboarding/review_actions.md beside this file in the repo.

MODULE 01 Basics

Why Nucleus exists, and where DP sits

Two loops share one set of stores and one per-user model. We are the refinery in the slow loop — and one day the fast loop will call us too.

Everything in Nucleus v0 is one of two loops:

Learn loop — background, nightly-ish · why the product exists
user's day→ recording :8084→ C1 DATA PROCESSING :8085→ C2 storage /context→ C10 continuum (nightly train)→ C5 model directory
Serve loop — interactive, seconds · what makes it usable today
user asks→ input / QueryBuilder⇄ C8* DATA PROCESSING (same pipeline)↩ QueryBuilder→ C3 inference (per-user model)→ C9 output

Our seat in the learn loop is load-bearing: recording captures, storage keeps, continuum trains — but the content of what gets trained is decided by what we emit. A chunk of Tuesday-morning audio becomes a transcript record; a chunk of screen video becomes a caption record and an on-screen-text record. Storage renders those records into the day-log; continuum trains on the day-log's text. Nothing else in the system produces training content.

Our seat in the serve loop doesn't exist yet — deliberately. C8* is the designed-but-unbuilt contract: when a user pastes a screenshot into chat, the QueryBuilder will normalize it through this same pipeline, synchronously, so interactive requests and the life stream speak one dialect. The inline ingest path is deliberately kept alive as C8's skeleton (more in module 10).

The one-dialect principleThe model must always see data in one voice. If the training data says Screen shows a Gmail compose window… and the interactive path said <image>, the model would meet two different worlds. One code path for both entry points is a charter-level commitment, not an optimization.

MODULE 03 Fundamentals

C1 in, C2 out — the two promises we live between

Everything this service does happens between two wire shapes. Learn them cold and half the codebase explains itself.

C1 — what arrives consumed

Recording pushes us a C1 raw-stream envelope for every few seconds of captured life. Two things matter before the shape: the bytes are already durable (recording PUT them to storage /raw first — "blob-first", so blob_ref can never dangle), and delivery is at-least-once — the same envelope may arrive twice, and that must be harmless. The full field-by-field anatomy lives in the recording field guide; what we care about:

  • chunk_id is the dedup key: the client mints it once and it never changes across retries, and that constancy is where our whole idempotency story starts.
  • (stream_id, sequence) is dense and zero-based, so any break — including a first-seen value above zero — means a lost chunk. Our continuity tracker turns breaks into visible gaps, and recording's report cross-checks them live.
  • blob_ref says where the bytes live and blob_sha256 lets us verify them end to end. The ref is opaque: we never parse it or infer a path from it — we ask storage.
  • modality is one of audio | video | image | text. One envelope shape serves all four, which is why the vision build never had to reshape the wire.
  • t_start and t_end are the chunk's span of the user's life in RFC3339 UTC — the time spine every downstream alignment leans on.
  • device_tz, device_utc_offset_minutes and device_location carry the civil-time provenance the founders assigned to the capturing device when they split timezone custody (D17). We copy them verbatim, and DP performs zero timezone logic, ever — no deriving, no validating, no guessing.

C2 — what leaves produced

For every unit of meaning a chunk contains, we emit one C2 processed record to storage /context. Click any field:

The mirror trap (D17's scar)The C2 shape is defined three times: the JSON Schema in product/contracts/ (source of truth), DP's pydantic mirror, and storage's pydantic mirror — and both mirrors are extra="forbid". Add a field to the schema without touching both mirrors and the system fails closed. That is deliberate: better a loud rejection than two services silently disagreeing about a shape. When you edit a contract: ARCHITECTURE first, then the schema, then both mirrors, same change.

C8 — the door that isn't built yet

The third contract on our charter is C8: the same pipeline offered synchronously to input's QueryBuilder. Nothing is built — but the decision that shaped today's code is that the inline ingest handler (module 05) is C8's skeleton: one shared process_chunk core plus ~40 lines of HTTP mapping. When C8 lands, it gets its own surface over the same core. Keep that in mind whenever someone proposes deleting the inline path "now that async exists" — it was proposed, and refused, on exactly this ground.

MODULE 04 Fundamentals

Identity & dialect — the one hash that holds the system together

Almost every hard guarantee in DP reduces to one line of code: record_id = sha256(chunk_id ␀ pipeline_version [␀ discriminator]). Understand its three inputs and you understand the service.

chunk_id — which moment

Ties the record to one captured span of life. Never changes across retries, so redelivery hits the same record.

pipeline_version — which dialect

Names how the record was produced — backends, prompts, config. Change how, and the id forks: reprocessing lands beside the old record, never over it.

discriminator — which unit within the chunk

One chunk can yield several records (a caption and its OCR). The discriminator keeps siblings distinct — and it's "" (absent) in the plain 1:1 case.

Why a hash at all? Storage's /context write is a blind upsert on record_id. Because the id is a pure function of its inputs, processing the same chunk twice writes the same id — the second write lands on the first. That single property converts recording's at-least-once delivery into an exactly-once effect, makes kill -9 recovery safe, and makes reprocessing history a version bump instead of a migration.

The ␀ between the partsComponents are joined with a NUL byte before hashing. Without a separator, ("ab","c") and ("a","bc") would hash identically — the classic composite-key collision. Also load-bearing: an empty discriminator is skipped entirely, so audio's 1:1 id is byte-identical to the id from before discriminators existed. Nothing ever re-keyed. (app/pipeline.py:33-46)

Play with the dialect

The dialect composer below is a faithful model of how pipeline_version is assembled from your environment knobs — base fragment from the primary stage, +fragments from mutate stages and providing sidecars, in a deterministic order. Flip knobs and watch which records fork and which hold steady.

records this chunk emits

Forward-only, by designA pipeline_version bump never repairs the past. Old records stay in /context; new ones land beside them, and storage's day-log picks one dialect per record (latest ingest_time wins). There is a cost, and the founders accepted it in writing when they moved the training window onto storage's ingest clock (D18): the same lived moment can train twice, in two dialects. Suppressing that would also suppress the correction — and the correction wins.

MODULE 06 Machinery

The stage graph — every step is a drop-in file

Since v1 (2026-07-20), each processing step is one file under app/stages/<modality>/ declaring what it is, what it needs, and what it may touch. The executor turns that into a concurrent DAG. Adding a step means adding a file — zero shared-core edits.

Three kinds of stage, one law each

primary emits the main records

Exactly one enabled primary per modality. Produces the modality's answer to "what happened in these bytes" via assemble(), which always runs last. Its version_fragment is the base dialect. Can never be best_effort. Example: ASR, clipcap.

mutate edits the primary in place

Declares writes ⊆ the primary's mutable_slots and must fork the dialect — its enabledness is version_fragment != "", so it physically cannot mutate without re-keying. Overlapping mutates are chained by (order, name), never concurrent. Example: diarization filling speakers.

sidecar adds its own records

Emits extra records under its own discriminator, or provides named slots downstream stages consume. Cannot even read the primary's mutable slots — the executor never hands it the reference. A sidecar that provides must fork the dialect (rider R1). Example: translate, acoustic, screentext.

The graphs as they exist today

What the executor guarantees

  • Readiness, not levels. Each stage is an asyncio task awaiting exactly its dependencies' futures — a slow sidecar never delays an independent stage (stagegraph/executor.py).
  • Capability-scoped slots. Every stage runs against a SlotView: in Python, holding a reference to a mutable object is the power to mutate it, so illegal access is prevented by never handing out the reference. A violation raises SlotAccessError at the offending line.
  • Commit on success only. A stage's slot outputs merge into the shared context only after it succeeds — a half-run stage contributes nothing, like a rolled-back transaction.
  • Required failure fails the chunk (all siblings cancelled and awaited, the real leaf exception re-raised); best_effort failure skips its cone and counts a metric. Nothing required may sit downstream of a best_effort stage — "its promise would be hollow."
  • Duplicate discriminators are terminal. Two units sharing one would share a record_id and silently clobber each other in /context (executor.py:393-401).
  • The dialect composes deterministically: base fragment + mutate fragments in chain order + remaining fragments sorted — so declaration order can never perturb identity.

History: why the fingerprint guard diedv1 checked slot integrity with an end-of-run fingerprint — and the hardening review proved it order-dependent: an illegal write landing before the mutate cohort finished was baked into the reference snapshot and missed. The fix was structural, not a patch: delete the guard, deny the reference (SlotView). When you find yourself verifying after the fact, ask whether you can make the violation impossible instead.

MODULE 07 Machinery

The audio pipeline — real, end to end

Audio is the modality that has carried real user data since day one: phone-mic and tab audio through faster-whisper into /context, verified on real hardware in the capture alpha.

ASR — the primary

  • ASR_BACKEND=mock is the default, and it is deliberate: no GPU, no torch, byte-stable output the tests can pin. The real path is faster_whisper, which was smoke-tested green on node-7 and stands on the beta fleet today.
  • The VAD gate (ASR_VAD=1, default on) runs voice-activity detection before decoding. Whisper's classic failure is hallucinating plausible sentences from silence; with the gate, an all-silence chunk yields an honest empty transcript — still a valid C2 documenting its span. This dialect change is exactly why the version bumped asr-fw-v0 → asr-fw-v1.
  • ASR_LANGUAGE pins the language — the beta fleet pins en after the first real phone session: auto-detect on faint room audio hallucinated Hindi/Korean-script text. Deliberately a runtime knob, not a dialect fork: same-language reprocessing must stay an idempotent upsert.
  • Backends speak chunk-relative seconds; only the stage layer knows wall-clock, clamping offsets into the span and mapping to RFC3339. One conversion point, no backend can spill outside its chunk.

Why off-by-default beats mock-by-default hereA deliberate deviation from "mock is the default": if mock diarization were on by default, the default dialect would carry +diar-mock-v1 and every byte-identity test would break. So capabilities ship off; mock is the no-GPU backend you get when you deliberately turn one on.

The single-resolver invariant — memorize this oneDiarize's enabledness and its version fragment derive from one resolver. If they could diverge, a diarized record could be written under the undiarized dialect — minting the same record_id as the pristine transcript and silently overwriting it via the idempotent upsert. A typo in DIARIZE_BACKEND resolves to off in both places: never fork, never collide. Every backend switch in the service follows this pattern.

Traps the real backends taught us

  • torch ≥ 2.6 flipped torch.load to weights_only=True, breaking pyannote's checkpoints. The fix is a scoped override restored in finally — never a global monkeypatch.
  • torchaudio's soundfile backend can't demux webm/opus — pyannote input is ffmpeg-pre-decoded to 16 kHz mono WAV. ffmpeg on PATH is the one system dependency every real audio backend shares.
  • pyannote signals auth failure by returning None, not raising — and two gated HF repos must both be accepted. The code checks and raises with instructions.
  • Both discoveries came from running the backends on node-7, not reading them. The house phrase is "exercised vs correct-by-inspection" — and the code labels each honestly.

The clip path, stage by stage

  • clipprep — two ffmpeg passes, VFR-safe. Pass A decodes once and emits a 32×32 binarized change map every 2 s (binarize per-pixel first, then area-average — a whole-frame mean would divide one line of typing to zero). Pass B re-extracts exactly the frames Pass A provably decoded, selected by exact PTS — never round(t×fps), because macOS ScreenCaptureKit only emits frames when pixels change and a constant-rate assumption dead-letters healthy chunks.
  • The delta gate classifies each accumulated change map: peak ≤ 8 → IDLE · peak > 40 (or spread > 32) → LAYOUT · else TEXT. Any non-IDLE event triggers an OCR read; a floor timer guarantees one read per 120 s even on a static screen. The floor on flat frames is exactly 2/255 — a scaler artifact, guarded so it can never accumulate into a false event.
  • screentext asserts the OCR sidecar's model SHAs against /health before reading anything (a swapped model file fails loudly, never silently in the corpus), reads the delta-selected frames at native width, then post-processes DP-side: confidence gate → reading order → secret redaction (regex for AWS/GitHub/Slack keys, PEM blocks, Luhn-checked card numbers → [redacted:secret]) → dedup → char budget. Always emits exactly one ocr record — empty text when nothing legible, because record presence is the coverage signal.
  • clipcap makes one multi-image VLM call: K = clamp(⌈span/2.5s⌉, 2, 12) frames at 768 px (exactly 360 Qwen3-VL vision tokens each), OCR text injected as a labelled input block, the task last so an on-screen echo of the instructions can't hijack the parse. Renders {app} — {activity}. {description}, single line, truncated on a sentence boundary to the char budget.
  • The parse ladder absorbs whatever the VLM returns — clean JSON, fenced, truncated, wrong keys, prose — through seven rungs. A non-empty reply never raises; an empty reply always raises, so the chunk redelivers instead of a hollow record landing forever.

The char budget is a training dose, not formattingVIDEO_CHARS_PER_SECOND=22 (16 caption / 6 OCR) caps every record at rate × span. Downstream, continuum's acquisition was measured falling 3.2× when chars-per-block rose 3.7× — the model learns per retelling per unit of text. Caption length is a correctness knob. Related: at legacy caption volumes, the day-log's 6,000-char block excerpt truncated 100% of the OCR channel away — the clip path's budget keeps 45% headroom.

The OCR sidecar — a service within the service

sidecars/ocr/ is a separate loopback-only HTTP process with its own venv. Why: PaddleOCR's stack needs numpy<2.4; DP runs 2.5.1 — irreconcilable in one venv, so the dependency is quarantined behind a process boundary and the model becomes a file/URL swap. The bake-off harness proved PP-OCR@1728 reads synthetic macOS frames at 0.988 recall / 0.070 CER — and honestly recorded that the real gate (O-2: ~200 hand-labelled real frames) is still unmet, so production ships VIDEO_OCR_BACKEND=mock. A controlled ablation also proved the codec (CRF 28) costs ≈nothing at 1728 px — resolution, not compression, is the lever, which is why recording captures screens at its 1728 px cap and DP OCRs them with no further resample.

Cutover discipline — read before ever flipping VIDEO_PIPELINE(1) Never rolling-restart across a dialect change — drain and replace, with the dp_pipeline_dialect mixed-fleet alert as tripwire. (When this runbook was written, a mixed fleet could double-count the same seconds inside one training block; D18's one-dialect day-log rule has since closed that worst case, but a mid-flip corpus and reprocess churn remain reasons to keep the discipline.) (2) Rollback restores behaviour, not the corpus — vidclip records persist; no delete primitive exists until storage ships E-2. (3) Set DP_DIALECT_FREEZE=1 during the flip window so redeliveries serve stale receipts instead of mass-reprocessing.

MODULE 09 Governance

The record-vs-mutation law — when a signal earns a record

The service's constitution, ratified 2026-07-25. It answers the question every new stage forces: does this signal become a record, edit a record, or nothing? It is executable — CI enforces it — and this section teaches you to apply it.

The invariant. A C2 record is one independently-placeable, independently-labelable, independently-losable claim about a span of the user's life. Placement is t_start alone; labeling is content.kind alone; identity is the record_id hash; loss is per-stage. A signal deserves a record iff it needs its own place, its own label, or its own failure. A signal that makes an existing record's declared structure more precise is a mutation. A signal about how a claim was obtained is an enrichment — or nothing.

Five ordered tests — first match wins

TestQuestionIf it fires
T1 DERIVABLEPure function of this chunk's bytes + config?No → not a DP record. Cross-chunk work is continuum's.
T2 REACHABLEReaches a consumer that exists today?No → do not emit. Store nothing you cannot spend. (enrichments is read nowhere yet.)
T3 SPINEThe modality's answer to "what happened in these bytes"?PRIMARY unit(s); its fragment is the base dialect.
T4 EDITSChanges bytes a record already claims?Structure-fill → MUTATE (declares writes, mandatory fragment). String-change → FORBIDDEN: refine inside the stage, or fork pipeline_version.
T5 CHANNEL/SPANOwns a pinned kind / day-log line, or an independently addressable span?NEW RECORD with its own discriminator.

Fallthrough → enrichment (subject to T2) or plain stage input. Five riders sharpen it — the two you'll hit first: R1 (a sidecar that provides a slot must fork the dialect — a provided slot exists only to change someone else's bytes) and R3 (the dialect states the attempted pipeline; absence is diagnosed by record presence + a metric, never a fabricated placeholder — and continuum must never infer "no on-screen text" from an absent OCR record).

Enforced in three layers, deliberately

  • Registration raise (stagegraph/stage.py) — the structural half: a providing sidecar with no fragment resolver can't even register.
  • The CI matrix (tests/test_emission_law.py) — a 10-configuration environment matrix (including deliberate typos) re-checks every rule, and runs once more with the exemption list emptied to prove video/keyframes is the only violation in any configuration. No second stage can slide under the legacy carve-out silently.
  • Graph resolution (per chunk) — config-shaped errors that need live settings.

MODULE 10 Machinery

Crash safety — how the service refuses to lie

The hardening story in four acts: the async wire (D16), the durable journal, fairness, and isolation. The common thread: loss may be over-reported, never hidden.

Act 1 — the async wire, and the invariant that shaped it (D16)

Inline processing means recording's push blocks on ASR latency — a fully loaded chunk can lawfully outlive recording's delivery timeout. The fix is obvious (ACK 202, process on workers) and the trap is subtle: the pre-implementation review caught that a naive 202 would let an accepted-then-lost chunk read clean in recording's gap report — silent loss on the exact surface built to prevent it. The ratified fix is an invariant, not a feature: dp_acked=1 ⇔ the C2 is durably written. DP's /continuity gained additive processed + dead_lettered sets; recording confirms only on processed, and its /redrive re-pushes anything accepted-but-unconfirmed — free, because dedup makes re-pushes idempotent.

Act 2 — the durable journal (kill −9 is a test case)

  • Durable before ACK. The full C1 is written to SQLite (WAL, BEGIN IMMEDIATE — recording's proven ledger pattern) inside the claim, before the 202 leaves the process. The ACK never lies: if the box dies a microsecond later, startup finds the row and re-drives it.
  • Journal-before-dedup on success: the processed receipt lands before the in-memory dedup map — a crash between them re-drives an already-written chunk into a harmless idempotent upsert.
  • Epoch fencing. Every accept bumps the row's epoch; a stale worker finishing after a redelivery re-accepted the chunk no-ops instead of clobbering the fresh attempt. (The processed insert is deliberately unguarded — if C2s were written, the receipt is true.)
  • Rehydration keystone: at boot, still-pending rows count as seen coverage, so a restart can never fabricate a gap out of a chunk merely waiting for re-drive. This closed the one deferred caveat of the async slice — DP-restart amnesia reading intact history as gaps.
  • Crash-loop bounding with evidence: a poison chunk that crashes the service on every re-drive dead-letters after DP_REDRIVE_MAX_ATTEMPTS — and the counter charges only chunks whose processing actually started, after a review found the per-restart version dead-lettered fifty innocent bystanders.
  • Stale receipts aren't served: a redelivery of a chunk processed under an old dialect reprocesses version-forward — unless DP_DIALECT_FREEZE=1 holds receipts steady while a two-dialect fleet drains.

Act 3 — fairness without head-of-line blocking

INGEST_MODALITY_LIMITS=video=2 caps concurrent video so a burst can't starve audio. The v1 design (dequeue, then wait for the permit) head-of-line-blocked a whole worker — worse than no limit, and the reason the knob shipped with a warning label. The rewrite takes the permit atomically at dispatch, scanning past capped jobs to the first eligible one; retriers waking from backoff hold reservations so freed permits can't be barged away. Review even reproduced a starvation bug in the fix itself — the same-tick rescan always stole the freed permit from a parked retrier.

Act 4 — subprocess isolation (blast radius = 1 chunk)

INGEST_ISOLATION=subprocess (opt-in) runs exactly the Processor step in a killable child. Two failure classes an in-process pool structurally cannot contain: a segfault/native-OOM in model code kills one child instead of the service, and a drain-timeout cancel SIGKILLs the ghost computation a CPython thread could never stop. Blob fetch, C2 assembly, journal, and dedup stay in the parent. Known costs, documented: per-chunk process start, child-side metrics invisible to the parent registry, and no wall-clock kill for a hung child yet.

The adversarial-review cultureEvery slice here ended with a fan-out of adversarial reviewer agents (18 for async, 47 for hardening) and each round found real bugs in the new code — retry starvation, an event-loop stall in spawn isolation, the sidecar slot-clobber hole. The lesson the founders keep restating: green suites prove the paths they exercise. Reviews hunt the paths they don't.

MODULE 12 Machinery

We emit; platform watches (once it builds the watcher)

The founders split observability once, for everyone (D9): every service instruments itself, and platform runs the one shared Prometheus + Grafana. Our emission half shipped (M8). The shared backbone is still unbuilt — no founder has a Grafana URL yet.

  • Zero-dependency by choice: app/metrics.py is an in-house Prometheus-text registry plus a pure-ASGI middleware that never touches response bodies — so the headless CI suite stays dependency-free and exact-body assertions keep holding.
  • Counters are seeded to zero at startup because in this registry a never-incremented counter renders nothing, and Prometheus reads a missing series as "never happened" rather than "0 so far".
  • Pull-time gauges read live state at scrape: queue depth, journal counts, continuity aggregates, and dp_pipeline_dialect — whose mixed-fleet alert (shorthand: count by (modality) > 1; the replica-robust form lives at the declaration site in main.py) is the tripwire against a rolling deploy running two dialects at once.
  • Route templatization bounds label cardinality: /continuity/<id> collapses to one series.
  • The dashboard JSON (dashboards/data-processing.json) covers ingest rate, queue depth, dead-letters, 503s, retries, stage p95, dedup hits, VAD-empty rate — the WS-VC video families don't have panels yet (a tidy first contribution).

Metric truths worth knowingUnder subprocess isolation, child-side increments are invisible to the parent registry — the load-bearing per-unit counters were deliberately moved parent-side. dp_partial_write_total is the only signal that a caption landed without its OCR sibling (the A-4 window). And dp_caption_ungrounded_quote_total currently measures an empty set — mock captions carry zero double-quotes — its widened scorer exists in scripts/prompt_ab.py and wiring it is an open follow-up.

Wall-clock kill + warm child pool medium
