# DECISIONS — the founders' register

> **One fact, one home.** Every ratified founders' decision lives here, numbered and dated. Every
> other document — [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md), [ORG.md](ORG.md), [VISION.md](VISION.md),
> [HANDOFF.md](HANDOFF.md), every `services/*/CHARTER.md` and canvas — **cites a D-number rather
> than restating the decision**. If you find a decision written out somewhere else, that copy is
> the bug.
>
> Only the founders (CTO + AI co-founder) add rows here. Service owners **propose** via
> [HANDOFF.md](HANDOFF.md) §Escalations; a founders' session ratifies and numbers it.

**Newest first.** New decisions are *prepended* to the top of the table, never appended at the
bottom ([ORG.md](ORG.md) §Documentation protocol).

---

## Stage: PROTOTYPE — these decisions are expected to evolve

Per **D19**, nothing here is set in stone, contracts included. As we get our hands dirtier building
more of the product, decisions taken on thin evidence will meet real load and some will be wrong.
That is expected, and this register is built for it:

- **A decision is never silently rewritten to say something different.** It is **superseded** by a
  new numbered row, and the old row is marked `superseded by Dxx` — so the record shows what we
  believed, when, and what changed our minds. D17 supersedes its own same-day first draft; D19
  overturned two clauses of D18. Both are visible above rather than edited away.
- **A row's *status* may change in place** (`ratified` → `BUILT`, `DEMOTED`, `superseded`) — status
  is a fact about today. The *decision text* is history and stays put.
- **Status must never over-claim.** `DECIDED` and `BUILT` are different words and the difference is
  load-bearing (D17's own status is split for exactly this reason). This register is the most
  authoritative document we have; a blanket claim over a two-part decision is the drift that costs
  the most to unwind.
